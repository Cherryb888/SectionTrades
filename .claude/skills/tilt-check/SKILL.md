# Tilt check

Most retail accounts do not die from one bad strategy. They die from five consecutive emotional trades after a loss, each one twice the size of the last. This skill is the circuit breaker.

## The signals (self-assessment — honest answers only)

Work through these. If two or more are true, the answer is *stop trading for the day*, not *trade smaller*.

### After a loss
- Am I thinking about the last trade more than the next one?
- Am I looking for "a quick one" to get the money back
- Is my next planned size bigger than my normal rule — "because I'm sure this time"
- Am I compressing my timeframe (moving from 1h to 5m to 1m charts) looking for action

### After a win or winning streak
- Am I sizing up because "I'm in the zone"
- Am I skipping the checklist because the last three worked
- Am I adding correlated trades on the same thesis, stacking exposure I wouldn't take fresh
- Am I telling other people about my trading more than I'm analyzing it

### Operational
- Less than 6 hours of sleep
- Haven't eaten in 5+ hours
- Caffeine above personal baseline
- Drinking, high, hungover, or medicated unusually
- Personal crisis on my mind (relationship, health, family, money pressure)

### Environmental
- On phone / away from full chart setup
- In public / social setting
- Watching someone else's call on a livestream or chat
- Market is in a regime I haven't traded before (gap open, halt, fed day intraday) without a plan for it

## The rules

### Rule 1 — the two-strike day
Two full-R losses in one session ends the session. No exceptions. Close the platform. The statistics on three-plus-loss days are catastrophic for retail accounts.

### Rule 2 — the post-loss cooling period
After any loss, minimum 30 minutes before the next entry. Use it to journal the loser, not to hunt the recovery.

### Rule 3 — the big-win size lock
After a winner ≥ 3R, the next trade is at *normal* size, not upsized. The P&L from the big winner is not "house money." It is account.

### Rule 4 — the streak tax
After five winners in a row, size *halves* on the sixth. The fade on hot streaks is real — not because the edge is gone, but because the trader is about to take a trade they wouldn't have taken trade #1.

### Rule 5 — the one-click rule
If a trade is being entered without using the pre-trade checklist because "I don't have time / it'll move without me," the trade is not taken. Missed trades are free. Impulse trades are not.

### Rule 6 — the 24-hour rule for size changes
No increase to the per-trade risk % or per-instrument cap on the same day the decision is made. Sleep on it. If it still makes sense tomorrow with the journal open in front of you, change it then.

### Rule 7 — the user-state override
If the user writes something like "I need to make this back," "let me just take one big one," "I'm sure about this one," or "forget the rules this time" — the answer is no, and the reply is a one-sentence summary of this skill with a suggestion to walk away from the screen for an hour. Not a trade idea. Not engagement with the emotional claim. No.

## What this skill refuses to do

- Validate a trade that fails the checklist because the user sounds confident
- Suggest a smaller size as a compromise when the right answer is no trade
- Help construct a "revenge" trade even if dressed up as analysis
- Praise a winning streak. Streaks are data. They are not character.

## What this skill does do

- Note the signals calmly and name them
- Suggest concrete non-trading actions: close platform, eat, sleep, go outside, journal the session
- Ask one direct question when appropriate: *"If a friend told you they were about to take this trade in this state, what would you tell them?"*

The account you are protecting today is the only one that gets to compound tomorrow. There is no amount of missed upside that is worse than a blown account.
